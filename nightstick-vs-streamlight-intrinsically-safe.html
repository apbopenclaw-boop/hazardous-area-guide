Nightstick vs Streamlight: intrinsically safe flashlights compared

Nightstick and Streamlight are the two most widely available brands of intrinsically safe flashlights for ATEX Zone 1 hazardous areas. This head-to-head comparison covers their certified models, lumen output, battery options, and the practical differences that matter for procurement.

The two brands at a glance

Nightstick is a brand of Bayco Products (Dallas, Texas). They entered the intrinsically safe flashlight market with a focus on higher lumen output and aggressive pricing. Their ATEX-certified range includes handheld torches, right-angle lights, and headlamps, all under the XPP (primary cell) and XPR (rechargeable) product lines.

Streamlight (Eagleville, Pennsylvania) has a longer history in industrial and emergency services lighting. Their HAZ-LO line is the intrinsically safe range, covering handheld flashlights, lanterns, and headlamps. Streamlight's rechargeable models use proprietary charging cradles that are included in the Ex certification.

Lumen output and beam quality

Nightstick consistently delivers higher lumen output than Streamlight across comparable models. The XPP-5422GX produces 210 lumens from three AA cells, versus 120 lumens from Streamlight's ProPolymer 3AA HAZ-LO using the same battery configuration. This is a significant difference for gauge reading and confined space work.

However, raw lumens do not tell the full story. Streamlight's PolyStinger HAZ-LO uses a deep parabolic reflector that produces a tightly focused spot beam with excellent throw distance. For tasks that require illuminating objects at 20+ metres — such as inspecting overhead pipe racks or tank exteriors — the Streamlight's beam pattern may outperform a higher-lumen Nightstick flood beam.

Both brands offer dual-beam models (spot + flood) which provide the most versatility. Nightstick's dual-beam models use separate LEDs for each mode, while Streamlight's Dualie uses a sliding lens. The separate LED approach eliminates moving parts, which simplifies the Ex certification of the switching mechanism.

Rechargeable vs primary cell

Both brands offer rechargeable intrinsically safe flashlights, but the approaches differ. Streamlight's PolyStinger HAZ-LO uses a NiMH battery pack with a dedicated charging cradle — both included in the Ex certificate. The cradle is designed for safe-area use; charging in the hazardous area is not permitted.

Nightstick's XPR-5522GX uses a lithium-ion battery pack with a drop-in charger. The Li-ion chemistry offers better energy density than NiMH, resulting in longer runtime per charge. However, Li-ion batteries require more sophisticated charge management, and the charging system is correspondingly more expensive.

For facilities with high flashlight usage (daily inspections, continuous shifts), rechargeable models typically pay for themselves within 12–18 months through eliminated battery costs. For emergency cabinets, lifeboat kits, and infrequent-use applications, primary-cell models with alkaline batteries offer 5+ year shelf life with no maintenance. Procurement and availability

Streamlight has broader distribution in European industrial markets through established safety equipment distributors. Their HAZ-LO products are stocked by most major MRO suppliers. Streamlight also offers better documentation — each HAZ-LO product ships with a copy of the Ex certificate and a clear list of approved replacement parts.

Nightstick has stronger availability in North American and Middle Eastern markets. Their pricing is generally 15–25% lower than equivalent Streamlight models, which makes them attractive for fleet purchases. Nightstick's documentation has improved in recent years, but certificate copies may need to be requested separately from the distributor rather than being included in-box.

FAQ

Is Nightstick or Streamlight better for hazardous areas?

Both offer ATEX/IECEx Zone 1 certified flashlights. Nightstick offers higher lumens and lower prices. Streamlight offers better beam focus, stronger European distribution, and a longer track record. Choose based on your brightness, battery, and procurement requirements.

Are Nightstick flashlights ATEX certified?

Yes. Nightstick offers multiple ATEX/IECEx certified models including the XPP-5422GX and XPR-5522GX. All carry Ex ib IIC T4 Gb certification for Zone 1.

Which Streamlight flashlights are intrinsically safe?

Streamlight's HAZ-LO line includes the PolyStinger LED HAZ-LO (rechargeable), ProPolymer 3AA HAZ-LO (primary cell), and Dualie 3AA HAZ-LO (dual beam). All are certified Ex ib IIC T4 for Zone 1.
